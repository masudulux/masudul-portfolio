A Bidding Strategy and Tiny Tweaks Transformed Truck Hiring Prices

Showing truck owners where their bid ranks, without revealing amounts, and capping edits at five. Customer-care calls fell 47% and hiring requests rose 10%.

Truck Lagbe

Introduction

Today, I'll share an interesting story about how a single bidding position transformed the entire truck market pricing structure. This story unfolds within the operations of Truck Lagbe Ltd., a company specializing in truck hiring facilitated through a unique bidding process.

The Bidding Process

In the Truck Lagbe Ltd. system, users needing a truck submit a request via an app, specifying their location. Meanwhile, truck owners place bids, setting their desired trip fares. Customers then select a bid that suits their preference, often opting for the lowest offer.

Challenges Encountered

However, this system presented two major challenges:

  1. Excessive Customer Care Calls: Truck owners often call to request additional benefits. A primary concern for them is knowing how much to bid to secure a trip.
  2. Rising Truck Fares: Customers complained about the continuous increase in truck fares, leading to a decrease in hiring requests on our platform.

The Innovative Solution

After much deliberation, we agreed upon a transformative solution.

Bidding Position

I have decided to display the position of a truck owner's bid in comparison to others while keeping the actual bid amounts (in BDT) hidden. This allowed truck owners to see how their bids ranked without knowing the exact figures placed by others.

Bid Edit Restrictions

Additionally, I have implemented a minor but crucial tweak: truck owners could edit their bid to improve their position, but only up to five times. This limitation prevented excessive system strain and stopped owners from minutely adjusting bids to guess competitors' truck owner prices.

Impactful Results

The outcomes of these seemingly minor adjustments were remarkable:

  • A significant 47% reduction in inbound calls to our customer care helpline.
  • Truck owners started lowering their bids to achieve a higher position in the bid list, standardizing market rates to a more reasonable level.
  • A 10% increase in customer requests for truck hiring on our platform.

Conclusion

This case study demonstrates the power of strategic adjustments in digital platforms. By fine-tuning our bidding process, we not only enhanced operational efficiency but also fostered a more competitive and fair marketplace. It's a testament to how small changes can lead to significant impacts in the digital world of commerce.
